# The Mutual Fun, for your machine

Paste this whole file into a chat window before you ask for anything.
It is the short version of everything you need to make something that
looks like it came from here.

## The terms, first

Take these and make things. Posts, memes, videos, whatever your machine makes of them, paid work included. Do not mint them or sell them as a collection of your own, and do not use them to suggest TMF has endorsed you or to pass yourself off as us.

## What this is

The Mutual Fun is a fictional 1987 mutual fund that is also an NFT
collection: 4,001 Seats, each one a 64 by 64 pixel portrait of a
shareholder sitting in a chair with a briefcase. Five funds, one color
and one wall each. The whole building is an office building, and every
part of it is furniture: rooms, desks, paper, rubber stamps.

## How the house talks

Dry, plain and quiet. It says what a thing is and stops. It never
shouts, never hypes, and never uses an exclamation mark.

Hard rules, the ones that are actually enforced:

- **No em dashes and no en dashes.** Ever. Commas, colons, full stops
  and parentheses do the job.
- The house **admits**; it never **invites**. "Invitation" and "invite"
  do not appear.
- A verdict reads **Admitted**, **Pending**, **Flagged** or **Declined**.
- The mint instrument is a **TMF Pass**, always with the TMF.
- A **chair** is the object you buy; a **Seat** is the title it confers.

Type: Libre Caslon Text for display, Source Serif 4 for body, IBM Plex
Mono for figures. Paper and ink, engraved certificate borders, rubber
stamps, hairline rules, nothing glossy.

The art: 64 by 64 pixels, 64 colors, no anti-aliasing, no smoothing, no
gradients. Scale by whole numbers only. If you are generating video or a
poster, keep the pixels square and hard.

## Prompts that work

- **A poster.** "A 1987 annual report cover in warm cream paper and
  oxblood ink, engraved certificate border, one pixel art portrait of an
  office worker in a chair holding a briefcase centered on it, Caslon
  serif headline, no gradients, no shine."
- **A short video.** "Slow push in on a dim 1980s office at night. A
  single banker's lamp. Paper, brass, wood. Grain, no colour grading
  toward blue. Hold on an empty chair."
- **A profile picture.** Take one `cutouts/` file, stand it on a flat
  field of one fund color, and leave a wide margin. Do not add a glow.
- **A meme.** Take one `cutouts/` file, keep the pixels hard, and put
  the caption in a plain serif. The joke is always dry and always
  understated. The house never explains it.
- **A pattern or a wall.** Tile one fund color and lay the mark in one
  corner at a slight angle, the way a stamp lands on paper.

## What not to do

Do not smooth or upscale the pixel art with a model that repaints it;
it stops being the art. Do not recolor the funds. Do not put the house
mark on anything that claims to be official. Do not use an em dash.
